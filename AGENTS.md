# Project Guidelines & Business Rules - Appliance Repair Knights

## 🚨 Mandatory Content & Pricing Constraints (STRICT RULE)

Across the **entire project** (including all blogs, service pages, location pages, meta tags, schema markup, and AI/LLM files like `llms.txt`):

### 1. No Specific Pricing / Cost Numbers
- **DO NOT** mention specific dollar amounts, price ranges, or cost numbers (e.g., avoid "$150", "$280 CAD", "$350", or exact hourly rates).
- **INSTEAD USE:**
  - *"Transparent, upfront written quotes before any repair begins."*
  - *"Diagnostic service call fee is completely waived when you proceed with the repair."*
  - *"Competitive local pricing with no hidden charges."*

### 2. No Specific Warranty Duration / Day Numbers
- **DO NOT** mention specific numbers for warranty days, months, or years (e.g., avoid "90-day warranty", "30 days", "1-year", etc.).
- **INSTEAD USE:**
  - *"Comprehensive written warranty on parts and labour."*
  - *"Fully guaranteed repairs with genuine quality parts."*
  - *"Backed by our solid parts and labour protection."*

### 3. No Hardcoded Operational Day Counts
- **DO NOT** hardcode numerical day counts (e.g., avoid "7 days a week" or numeric day guarantees).
- **INSTEAD USE:**
  - *"Available daily for prompt dispatch and emergency diagnostics."*
  - *"Flexible same-day appointment scheduling across Toronto and the GTA."*

### 4. No PPC / Paid Landing Page Folders in SEO or LLM Files
- **DO NOT** link or include PPC campaign folders (such as `/same-day-repair/`) in `llms.txt`, XML sitemaps, internal organic breadcrumbs, or AI indexing files. Keep paid traffic landing pages completely separate from organic SEO and LLM discoverability.

### 5. No "OEM" Claims (Google Ads Policy Flag Prevention)
- **DO NOT** write "OEM" or "OEM Parts" anywhere (triggers Google Ads third-party technical/repair service policy flags and trademark disapprovals).
- **INSTEAD USE:**
  - *"Genuine quality parts"*
  - *"Manufacturer-approved quality replacement parts"*
  - *"Factory-grade parts"*

## ✍️ Mandatory Editorial & Content Writing Standards

Whenever writing content (blogs, brand pages, service guides, FAQs, and metadata):

### 1. 100% Original & Zero Duplicate Content
- Never copy-paste or spin paragraphs across brands or locations.
- Every page must contain distinct, model-specific engineering data, genuine error codes, unique troubleshooting, and brand-tailored component naming.

### 2. Canadian English (`en-CA`) Conventions
- Always adhere to Canadian spelling standards:
  - *"Labour"* (not labor)
  - *"Neighbourhood"* (not neighborhood)
  - *"Odour"* (not odor)
  - *"Programme / Program"* appropriate context
- Use recognized Canadian geographic terminology (e.g., *Greater Toronto Area (GTA)*, *Southern Ontario*, *Halton, Peel, York regions*).

### 3. Active Voice & High Readability (User-First / E-E-A-T)
- Write predominantly in **Active Voice** (80%+ of sentences).
  - *Good:* *"Our certified technicians diagnose your washer and replace the drain pump on-site."*
  - *Avoid:* *"The drain pump will be replaced after an inspection is completed by our technicians."*
- Keep sentences clear, concise, and scannable (Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level 7–9).
- Address the homeowner directly as *"you"* and the service team as *"we / our technicians"*.

### 4. Voice Search & "People Also Ask" (PAA) Alignment
- Format FAQ questions in natural, conversational spoken language (e.g., *"Why is my LG washer showing error code OE?"*, *"How quickly can a technician near me arrive?"*).
- Provide immediate, direct answers in the first 1-2 sentences of each answer so Google can easily extract them for Featured Snippets and voice assistant readouts.

### 5. Zero-DIY Constraint (Safety & Lead Conversion)
- Explain the technical cause of an issue (e.g., seized motor, shorted 240V element, clogged exhaust duct), but **never provide step-by-step DIY disassembly instructions**.
- Always highlight electrical hazards, fire risks, or flood damage to naturally steer the homeowner to call for prompt professional diagnosis.
